Privacy policy

Last updated 16 November 2026

ContentFlow is a personal publishing tool that runs on its owner's own computer. It has no servers of its own, no user accounts and no analytics. This policy explains what it does with data it receives from Google when its owner connects a YouTube channel.

What ContentFlow accesses

With the owner's permission, granted on Google's consent screen, ContentFlow uses these Google permissions:

  • Manage your YouTube videos (youtube.upload): to upload videos the owner has prepared and approved.
  • View your YouTube account (youtube.readonly): to show which channel is connected and to read the channel's own videos and their public counts.
  • View YouTube Analytics reports (yt-analytics.readonly): to read the channel's own watch time for its videos.

How the data is used

The data is used only to publish the owner's videos and to show the owner how those videos perform, inside ContentFlow. It is not used for advertising, it is not sold, and it is not used to train any AI model.

Where the data is stored

Access tokens and channel statistics are stored only in ContentFlow's local database on the owner's computer. They are never sent to any server other than Google's own APIs.

Sharing

ContentFlow does not share Google user data with anyone.

Limited Use

ContentFlow's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Removing access and data

The owner can disconnect a channel inside ContentFlow at any time, which deletes its stored tokens. Access can also be revoked from your Google Account at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Deleting ContentFlow's local database removes everything it stored.

Contact

Questions about this policy can be raised as an issue on this site's repository.
